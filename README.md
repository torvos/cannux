Cannux is an effort to build a sovereign Canadian Linux distribution: an operating system developed openly, governed transparently, and designed with Canadian interests in mind.

The goal is not to reinvent everything from scratch, but to create a trusted, community-driven platform that Canadians can understand, audit, contribute to, and ultimately rely on.

> **Status: early days.** There is nothing to download yet. The project is gathering interest and choosing its upstream foundation. [Have your say in the poll.](https://github.com/torvos/cannux/discussions/1)

## Why

An operating system is one of the most fundamental layers of modern computing. It powers government services, research institutions, businesses, schools, and personal devices. Linux is already open source, but most major distributions are governed, funded, or hosted outside Canada.

Countries such as France and the Netherlands are already adopting, funding, and developing open source technology to support their digital sovereignty. Canada should be part of that conversation.

This is not about isolation. It is about resilience, transparency, and ensuring that Canadians have a meaningful role in the technologies they depend on every day.

## The plan

1. **Select a foundation.** Choose a mature Linux distribution as the upstream base, for stability, proven security practices, and a large software ecosystem. This is where we are now: [vote in the poll](https://github.com/torvos/cannux/discussions/1).
2. **Conduct a comprehensive review.** Systematically audit the code, dependencies, infrastructure, and build processes, to understand exactly what is included, where it comes from, and what risks exist.
3. **Build and verify.** Add the features, tools, and services needed for a complete operating system. Every change is reviewed, tested, and verified to the same auditing standard, so that trust is built in from day one.

## How to help

Cannux starts with a community.

- **Express your interest.** The most important step right now. Star the repo and join the [discussion](https://github.com/torvos/cannux/discussions/1). Developers, designers, security researchers, policy experts, writers, and system administrators are all welcome, as is anyone who believes Canada should have greater digital sovereignty.
- **Contribute.** As the project grows it will need help with development, security auditing, documentation, testing, infrastructure, design, translation, and community building.
- **Sponsor.** Once the project has momentum, sponsorship will help cover hosting, code audits, development infrastructure, and legal costs.

Questions? Email [cannux@torvos.ca](mailto:cannux@torvos.ca).

## The name and the mascot

**Can**ada + Li**nux** = **Cannux**. It is also a playful nod to "Canuck".

The mascot is **Tuque**, the Canadian cousin of Tux, the Linux penguin created by Larry Ewing. Tuque has a beaver's buck teeth and tail, and is dressed for a Canadian winter in a plaid shirt, suspenders, and the knit cap that gives the mascot its name.

## A note on GitHub

Yes, a sovereignty project hosted on GitHub. It is a practical compromise: GitHub is the largest platform for open source collaboration and makes it easy to start a project and attract contributors. As Cannux grows and secures funding, we intend to evaluate Canadian-hosted alternatives for source control, issue tracking, package distribution, and project infrastructure.
